Road calibration

Step 1 · road quad

1 · Lens correction

  1. Trace 2–3 lines that are straight in real life but look bent in the photo — a kerb, a roofline, a fence.
  2. Click 4–8 points along each, then End line.
  3. Spread them across the frame; lines near the edges carry the most information.
  4. Points can be dragged or nudged with the arrow keys afterwards; the lens re-solves itself.

A wide lens bows straight lines. Left uncorrected it biases every speed by several percent, differently depending on where in the frame the car was.

2 · Road quad

  1. Click 4 points on the road surface, going around the shape — not diagonally.
  2. Order: near-left, near-right, far-right, far-left.
  3. Pick things you can measure: lane-line ends, driveway corners, a hydrant, a pole base.

Keep clicking the photo exactly as it looks, bends and all. The picture is never straightened on screen — the lens correction from step 1 is applied to your clicks automatically. There is no corrected image to click on.

Click points at road level, where tyres touch. A point on a kerb top or a wall is above the road plane and will bias every speed.

Drag any point to move it. Click one to select, then use the arrow keys to nudge it a pixel at a time (hold Shift for 10), Delete to remove it, Esc to deselect. A magnifier follows the cursor — the frame is 4K shown small, so one screen pixel is several image pixels and dragging by eye alone is not precise enough.

3 · Real-world size

Where each clicked point sits on the ground, in metres. X across the street, Y along it. The origin can be anywhere — only the distances between the points matter. They need not form a rectangle.

Use the shortcut only if the four points really are a rectangle on the road — two points square across the near kerb and two square across the far one, the same distance apart on both sides. Where the kerb curves, measure each point instead.

4 · Road area

Click the outline of the drivable road, then close it. Tracks straying outside are rejected. Leave empty to use the whole frame.

5 · Check & save

The green grid is 1 m squares projected onto the road, bent back through the lens. It should lie flat along the road and stay square-ish, and its lines should follow the same curve the kerb does. If it fans out or skews, a point is misplaced.
